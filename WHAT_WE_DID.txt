Everything we did, in simple words
Last updated: 4 September 2026

-------------------------------------------------
1. WHAT YOU ASKED FOR
-------------------------------------------------

The "sender" side of iTantra, built in React Native:

  - Record voice from the microphone
  - Detect when someone starts and stops speaking
  - Convert speech to text, fully offline (no internet)
  - Wrap the text in a data packet
  - Send it out through a transport layer
  - Show it all on a good looking dark screen
  - Support 10 languages

4. HOW IT WORKS (STEP BY STEP)
-------------------------------------------------

Think of it as an assembly line. Sound goes in one end, a
finished message packet comes out the other.

  STEP 1 - LISTEN
     The microphone opens at 16000 samples per second, single
     channel. (Android's own logs confirm this is exactly what
     the phone delivers.)

  STEP 2 - CUT INTO SMALL PIECES
     The sound is chopped into equal chunks of 32 milliseconds.

  STEP 3 - IS THIS SPEECH OR SILENCE?
     Each chunk is checked. The detector learns what "quiet"
     sounds like in the room you are actually in, then flags
     anything clearly louder as speech.

  STEP 4 - FIND THE SENTENCE ENDING
     Once speech starts we keep collecting. When you go quiet
     for the chosen pause (600, 750 or 1000 ms), the sentence
     is treated as finished.

     Two details that matter a lot:
       - We keep a little audio from BEFORE you started
         talking, or the first letter gets chopped off.
       - A short breath mid-sentence does not cut you off.
         Only a real pause does.

  STEP 5 - TURN SPEECH INTO TEXT
     The collected audio goes to the offline speech engine.
     Which engine depends on the language (see section 6).

  STEP 6 - CLEAN UP THE TEXT
     Two safety nets, both explained in section 6.

  STEP 7 - BUILD THE PACKET
     Exactly the format you specified:
         id, senderId, timestamp, language,
         text, priority, isCompressed

  STEP 8 - SEND IT
     Handed to the transport layer. Right now that is a
     "mock" one that just logs. Real radio or Bluetooth can be
     dropped in later without changing anything else.


-------------------------------------------------
5. THE PRIORITY FEATURE
-------------------------------------------------

The app reads the message and decides how urgent it is, from
keywords - in ALL TEN languages.

  CRITICAL - emergency, fire, attack, आग, ନିଆଁ, തീ ...
  HIGH     - urgent, help, injured, मदद, ସାହାଯ୍ୟ, സഹായം ...
  MEDIUM   - report, status, position, स्थिति, ସ୍ଥିତି ...
  NORMAL   - everything else

We used a plain keyword list instead of AI on purpose: an
operator must be able to understand exactly WHY a message was
marked urgent.

6. THE BIG PROBLEMS WE HIT (AND HOW WE FIXED THEM)
-------------------------------------------------

This section is the real story of the project.

PROBLEM 1: "It says [Music] when I say nothing"
  The speech model was trained on film subtitles. Given silence
  it does not return nothing - it returns what a subtitle would
  have said, like "[Music]" or "Thanks for watching".
  FIX: audio that is less than 35% actual voice never reaches
  the engine at all, plus a filter for these subtitle phrases.

PROBLEM 2: "There is a delay when I press the button"
  The first press was loading the whole speech model before
  opening the microphone.
  FIX: the model now loads when the app starts, and the mic
  opens immediately without waiting for it.

PROBLEM 3: "Hindi is completely wrong"
  This was the hardest one, and the cause was a surprise.

  The Whisper engine stores text as raw bytes. English letters
  take 1 byte each, but Hindi letters take 3. The library we
  use converts them one piece at a time, so Hindi letters were
  arriving broken and being thrown away.

  We proved it by printing the individual pieces:
       ["ह","म","े","ं"," ","","","","र"]
                          ^^  ^^  ^^
                    three EMPTY pieces where words should be

  So it was never "the AI is bad at Hindi". It was a text
  handling bug, and NO bigger Whisper model could have fixed
  it. We wasted a download proving that.

  FIX (first attempt): switched to a different engine called
  Dolphin, which stores real Hindi letters instead of bytes.
  That fixed the broken text but created Problem 4.

  FIX (final): every Indian language now uses its own separate
  AI4Bharat model. This is what actually solved it properly.

PROBLEM 4: "It writes Hindi words in the wrong alphabet"
  The new engine guesses the language itself and sometimes
  guesses wrong - writing correct Bengali WORDS in the Hindi
  ALPHABET, or slipping into Arabic.

  FIX (first attempt): all Indian alphabets are laid out in the
  same order in Unicode, so converting between them is simple
  arithmetic. The app converts wrong-alphabet text back into the
  right one instead of deleting it. Text in a truly unrelated
  alphabet (Arabic, Russian) cannot be saved and is dropped.
  This code is still there as a safety net.

  FIX (final): giving each language its own model removed the
  guessing entirely. A model that only knows Tamil cannot
  mistake your speech for Bengali. This is why all ten
  languages work now.

PROBLEM 5: "The decoding takes seconds, it should be instant"
  Whisper writes out one letter at a time, which is slow.
  FIX: switched to "CTC" models, which do it in one shot.
       English went from 6600 ms to 500 ms.
       Hindi went from broken to 546 ms.

PROBLEM 6: "Indian languages stopped working entirely"
  A bug we introduced. When we started using different engines
  for different languages, the app remembered only ONE engine
  location. So asking for Hindi handed back the ENGLISH engine
  folder, which failed, and the app quietly gave up.
  It was invisible because the error was being swallowed.
  FIX: remember one location per engine, and always log
  failures loudly.

PROBLEM 7: "The mic stopped working for every language"
  Not a bug. You were on a phone call. Android gives a call
  exclusive use of the microphone.
  FIX: the app now says so clearly, instead of quietly
  switching to fake audio and pretending to listen.


-------------------------------------------------
7. WHAT IS STILL NOT PERFECT - PLEASE READ
-------------------------------------------------

  a) NO CAPITAL LETTERS OR FULL STOPS
     The fast engines produce plain lowercase text. This is the
     price of the big speed improvement (under a second instead
     of six seconds).

  b) ONLY ONE APP CAN USE THE MICROPHONE
     If you are on a call, or a voice recorder or assistant is
     running, recording will not work. The app now says so
     clearly instead of pretending to listen.
     BEFORE A DEMO: close other audio apps, and do not be on
     a call.

  c) THE TRANSPORT IS STILL A LOOPBACK
     "Sending" currently just hands the packet back to the
     receiving side on the same phone. That is enough to prove
     the whole chain works. Real radio or Bluetooth is a
     separate piece of work, and slots in without changing
     anything else.

  d) EACH INDIAN LANGUAGE NEEDS ITS OWN MODEL FILE
     About 188 MB each. You only need to install the languages
     you actually plan to use.

  e) DEVELOPMENT BUILDS RELOAD BY THEMSELVES
     While the developer server is running, saving a file
     reloads the app and can interrupt recording. For a demo,
     use a release build so this cannot happen.

-------------------------------------------------
8. HOW TO RUN IT
-------------------------------------------------

The app is ALREADY INSTALLED on your phone. To rebuild:

     export JAVA_HOME="$HOME/Library/Java/JavaVirtualMachines/temurin-17.jdk/Contents/Home"
     export ANDROID_HOME="$HOME/Library/Android/sdk"
     npx expo run:android

IMPORTANT: Java 17 is required. Your Mac defaults to Java 25,
which breaks the build.

To just start the server for an installed app:

     npx expo start --dev-client

BEFORE A DEMO: close other apps that use the microphone, and
do not be on a call. Android only lets one app record at a time.


-------------------------------------------------
9. WHICH ENGINE IS USED FOR WHAT
-------------------------------------------------

  SPEECH TO TEXT
    English            -> NeMo CTC             (about 64 MB)
    Each Indian lang   -> AI4Bharat
                          IndicConformer       (about 188 MB each)

  TEXT TO SPEECH (for the receive side)
    English, Hindi, Malayalam -> Piper voices
    Other Indian languages    -> MMS voices

To change any of these, edit:
     src/config/models.ts      (speech to text)
     src/config/ttsModels.ts   (text to speech)

===============================================================
SHORT VERSION
===============================================================

The whole system is built, installed and working offline on
your phone, in all 10 languages, both sending and receiving.

Every language now transcribes correctly in under a second.

The hardest bug was Hindi, and it turned out not to be an AI
accuracy problem at all - the library was breaking Hindi text
apart byte by byte. Two engine changes fixed it: first to a
different multilingual engine, and finally to a separate
AI4Bharat model for each Indian language. Giving each language
its own model removed all the guessing, and made everything
roughly ten times faster than where we started.

What remains is the real radio transport, which is deliberately
a separate piece of work and plugs in behind one interface.
